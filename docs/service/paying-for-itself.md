# Is the service paying for itself

**Screen: Service, first panel**

## Give it your figures

Under **What people are paid**, fill in:

| Field | What to enter |
|---|---|
| **Handyperson, per hour** | Gross hourly pay, before on-costs. On-costs are added for you |
| **Admin team, per hour** | Hourly pay of the people who take the bookings |
| **Overhead, per year** | Van, fuel, insurance and tools |
| **Charged, per hour** | What customers are charged. The current rate is shown underneath |

The grey figures in the boxes are examples, not saved values. Until you type your own, the answer reads **Cannot say yet**.

Change any figure and everything on the page moves with it. Nothing you type here changes a booking, a price or an invoice; it is a calculator.

## Choose the period

Select **Last 60 days**, **Last 120 days** or **Last year**. A shorter window reflects how the service runs now. A longer one smooths out holidays and quiet spells.

## Read the answer

| Shown as | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Covers its costs by** (an amount) a year | Income is above wages and overhead |
| **Short by** (an amount) a year | Income does not cover them |
| **Cannot say yet** | A figure is missing. Enter what people are paid |

Under the answer are four tiles:

| Tile | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Utilisation | The share of paid time spent on a customer job. It also shows the level needed to break even |
| Income | Work delivered, scaled to a year |
| Wages | The handyperson's pay plus on-costs, scaled to a year |
| Visits | Visits in the period, with the number of weeks and days worked |

## What this is built on

The last panel on the page shows the evidence: the period measured, days worked, chargeable and free quoting visits, hours in front of customers, admin hours, and the working day assumed. If the period is short or the number of days worked is small, treat the answer as a guide.
